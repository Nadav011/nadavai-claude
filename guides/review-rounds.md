# Review rounds: what is reviewed how, and what a real fault is

Read before a review round starts and before acting on its verdict. The rule itself, with Nadav's pick of 2026-11-13
and his words of 2026-11-14, is in `rules/working-style.md`, Second model ("Review rounds converge"). Everything here
is the agent's choice and detail, his to reverse. When the reviewer is not `nadavai:reviewer` (a provider through
`omc ask`), put the definition of a fault and a note, and in the four areas the instruction to read the whole change,
into its brief.

- His rule stays for work that is built or carried out: code, a migration, a data change, a message flow, a single message when a review of it runs (`rules/working-style.md`, Second model, first bullet; the message gate is his yes on the send), a filing, an operation an agent runs on a live system or on real people's data (a read or a copy included), and a rule, hook, skill or guide that agents act on (a rule text counts as in an area when it changes what an agent does there: a gate, a send, a charge, a data operation, or who reviews them). Each unit in those four areas is reviewed that way when it is built, before it ships or acts, and its reviewer also argues against the behaviour the document gave it, not only checks the unit against it.
- A document that work is built from (a plan, a spec, a work order, a handoff) gets one independent round, the reviewer arguing against it: its faults are fixed, the same reviewer checks those fixes only (that check is not a round). A fault still open after that check, one that the fixes themselves made included, is corrected in the sentences the check named, in one writing pass. That pass is not a round and no further check follows it. A fault that is only a detail of a piece not built next is named in that piece, and the rest of the document is kept until its turn. A fault that comes from a decision nobody made goes to him as a question (Requests), and his answer is written in as he gave it, whether or not a fault is still open after the check, and before anything is built from the document: the option text when he picked an option, or the words he typed when he did not. When the reviewer no longer exists (a restart, a limit), first continue it by its agent id (SendMessage to that agent; a provider run through `omc ask` has no agent id, so this step fails). If that fails, a fresh reviewer gets the first report and the text before and after the fixes, and checks those fixes only. Use the same kind when it can run; when it cannot, use the fallback order in Second model, and do not wait for the kind that failed. The document is then frozen and built from. What turns up later is fixed where it is built.
- A demo or a prototype that is thrown away, runs on invented data and reaches no real person is "everywhere else", also when its screens show money; one that reads real data, sends anything or is shown to a client as the product is reviewed as built work in its area.
- A real fault is a finding that, left as it is, harms a person, money or data, gets work built or carried out wrong (two readings that change what is built count), credits Nadav with a decision he did not make, puts a question to him without a fact he would weigh, or weakens an approval gate; a finding that is also a note is a fault. A note (a stale restatement, a wording preference, a detail of a piece not built next (carried to that piece, not fixed now), or one that a check already written in the piece catches) is fixed without another round, in the reviewer's wording where it gave one, and is not counted; a round with notes only is not a rejecting round.
- Simplifying keeps what the piece does for a person, because cutting a capability is a product decision (Requests), and the simplified piece is new work that gets its own review. In those four areas a reviewer reads the whole change in every round; elsewhere it may start from what changed since the round before. The per-round counts go into the next report to him, and the rounds go on.
