# Review rounds: what is reviewed how, and what a real fault is

Read before a review round starts and before acting on its verdict. The rule itself, with Nadav's pick of 2026-11-13
and his words of 2026-11-14, is in `rules/working-style.md`, Second model ("Review rounds converge"). Everything here
is the agent's choice and detail, his to reverse. When the reviewer is not `nadavai:reviewer` (a provider through
`omc ask`), put the definition of a fault and a note, and in the four areas the instruction to read the whole change,
into its brief.

- His rule stays for work that is built or carried out: code, a migration, a data change, a message flow, a single message when a review of it runs (`rules/working-style.md`, Second model, first bullet; the message gate is his yes on the send), a filing, an operation an agent runs on a live system or on real people's data (a read or a copy included), and a rule, hook, skill or guide that agents act on (any of those counts as in an area when it changes what an agent does there: a gate, a send, a charge, a data operation, or who reviews them). Each unit in those four areas is reviewed that way when it is built, before it ships or acts, and its reviewer also argues against the behaviour the document gave it, not only checks the unit against it.
- A document that work is built from (a plan, a spec, a work order, a handoff) goes through this order once, and is then frozen and built from:
  1. One independent round, the reviewer arguing against it and marking each finding a fault or a note.
  2. A fault that comes from a decision nobody made goes to him as a question at once (Requests). Nobody chooses for him in order to pass the check, and his answer is written in as he gave it, before anything is built from the document: the option text when he picked an option, or the words he typed when he did not.
  3. A fault that is only a detail of a piece not built next is noted in that piece, which is not detailed further until its turn. The other faults are fixed.
  4. The same reviewer checks those fixes only; that check is not a round. An agent reviewer is continued by its agent id (SendMessage). When it no longer exists (a restart, a limit), or the reviewer ran through `omc ask` and has no agent id, a fresh reviewer gets the first report and the text before and after the fixes, and checks those fixes only: the same kind when it can run, else the fallback order in Second model, without waiting for the kind that failed.
  5. A fault still open after that check, one that the fixes themselves made included, is corrected in the sentences the check named, in one writing pass. A fault of step 2's kind goes to him as step 2 says, and the pass does not choose for him. That pass is not a round, and no further check follows it.
  6. The document is frozen and built from. What turns up later is fixed where it is built.
- The final read by another vendor's model (`rules/working-style.md`, Second model, which carries his three picks, the areas and the order of readers; the mechanics here are the agent's). The rule under all of it: the version that merges, ships or acts is one that the other vendor's model has read.
  - When and who: after the unit's review (a fresh Opus in these areas) approved that version, after CI is green where CI runs, and after the trial ran on it where `production-change.md` gives one (steps 2 to 4); right before the merge or, with no merge, right before the unit ships or acts. In a message flow it comes before the replay that is shown to him; for a single message, a filing, or a change that waits for his yes under the money gate, before it is shown to him. The session that owns the unit starts it: a subagent or a Workflow agent returns the approved unit to its caller, unless a project procedure he approved gives a step this read.
  - A later change: a change that alters what the unit does, its commands, its target or its way back makes a new version. The unit's review approves it, the trial runs on it as `production-change.md` says, and the other vendor reads that version once. A change that alters none of those (a comment, a test, a rebase over unrelated files) brings no further read. Each version is read once; rounds on the same version are the second loop he declined. The record names the commit, script or commands that were read and that then ran.
  - What the reader gets: the change itself (the diff with the changed files, a migration or SQL, a template, a flow), the acceptance criteria, under an engineering action also the exact commands, the target and the way back with every secret left out, the question "what in this would harm a person, money or data, or does not do what the criteria say?", the definition of a fault and a note, and the words "read-only". It does not get the report of the unit's review or the author's conclusion.
  - Where it runs, and personal details: as `review-providers.md` says for every provider review (a new folder with only the material, the session's tokens out of the provider's environment). Identifying details of a real person (a name, contact details, an identity number, a bank account or card number, a row's id) are always replaced with invented ones; other personal details and a client's confidential terms are replaced only where that does not change what is checked. Each invented value is the same everywhere it appears, the criteria included, and a finding is mapped back onto the real text before anything is fixed. A point that only the real identifying details can check (a name's spelling, rows matched by a real phone) is checked by a second fresh Opus on the real text, and the report says which part each one read. The sentences on a message and on a filing below apply this to those two cases. A message is read on a copy whose identifying details are invented: the recipient's and every other real person's name, contact details, identity numbers and row ids in it; amounts, promises and the ask stay as in the draft. A filing is read on a copy whose names, identity numbers and amounts are invented: that copy is enough to check the form, the recipient and the logic, and a second fresh Opus checks that the real numbers and identity details agree with each other; the report says which part each one read.
  - A finding: a fault from this read stops the merge and the apply step while it is open; it does not replace the approval or the rejection of the unit's review. The fault is fixed, the new version goes back to the unit's review together with the finding, and it is then a later change (above). A finding the author believes is not real goes to the unit's review on the version the vendor read: the code changes only when that review says the finding is real, and when it says it is not, the report says why and the unit goes on. The author does not turn a fault into a note, and the round is counted like any other. A note whose fix alters nothing the unit does is fixed without the vendor reading again, and the version that carries the fix goes back to the unit's review: outside an engineering action that review checks the note's fix only and is not a round; under one it is a new review of the exact commit (`production-change.md`, step 1).
  - When the usual reader cannot be used: when no other vendor's model can run, a second fresh Opus (a new `nadavai:reviewer`, not the one that approved) gets the same things and reads in its place. When the unit's own review did not run on Opus, for whatever reason, the read is a fresh Opus if it can run by then, and otherwise the first vendor's model that did not review the unit. The read of a later version goes to the reader of the version before it when that reader was another vendor's model, can run and did not review this version; otherwise it is chosen as for a first read (the reader order in `working-style.md` and the sentences above); nothing waits for the first. When no reader this bullet allows can run, the read is a step that cannot be done: an engineering action does not go on to its apply step (`production-change.md` already says that a step which cannot be done stops and asks, naming the step); a message draft, a filing or a change behind his yes is shown to him with that fact; any other unit in these areas is held until a reader can run, and the session's status says which read is missing.
  - The record: the verdict goes where the verdict of the unit's review is (`production-change.md`, step 1), and the report names the reviewer and the model of another vendor that read the version that ran, or says that a second Opus read it and why.
- A demo or a prototype that is thrown away, runs on invented data and reaches no real person is "everywhere else", also when its screens show money; one that reads real data, sends anything or is shown to a client as the product is reviewed as built work in its area.
- A real fault is a finding that, left as it is, harms a person, money or data, gets work built or carried out wrong (two readings that change what is built count), credits Nadav with a decision he did not make, puts a question to him without a fact he would weigh, or weakens an approval gate; a finding that is also a note is a fault. A note (a stale restatement, a wording preference, a detail of a piece not built next (carried to that piece, not fixed now), or one that a check already written in the piece catches) is fixed without another round, in the reviewer's wording where it gave one, and is not counted; a round with notes only is not a rejecting round.
- Simplifying keeps what the piece does for a person, because cutting a capability is a product decision (Requests), and the simplified piece is new work that gets its own review. In those four areas a reviewer reads the whole change in every round; elsewhere it may start from what changed since the round before. The per-round counts go into the next report to him, and the rounds go on.
