# my-method: is the way we work right, or doubled and wasted

Nadav has many sessions working without a break on demos, specs, plans and review rounds. He asks whether all of it serves the product itself: what is right, what happens twice, what need not happen, and what should happen at another time. He asked it in six sessions on 2026-11-14 and each answered for its own corner; the question is the same everywhere. How much to do is already his answer, given that day in the same words to four of them: «אני תמיד רוצה הכי מלא שיש אבל בלי כפילויות או דברים שסתם מעכבים אותנו», and «סגור לגמרי אבל בדרך הנכונה והאמיתית». So nothing he asked for is dropped here: each thing is closed in full, once, in the place where it stays.

1. Measure before you answer, and say where each number came from. For this session's work, or for what he named: how much of it is in the product itself (commits on the app's main branch, units of the plan that are built and checked, screens that work) and how much is around it (rounds of a demo, a spec, a plan or a work order; reviews; drafts; handoff files; tools not yet used on the real thing). `token-audit 1` shows what the last day cost and who spent it.
2. Sort every stream of work that is running or planned into four, each with its number:
   - It carries into the product, and how: a decision of his, a rule that becomes a test, an acceptance line, design values, a finding about real behaviour.
   - It happens twice: the same thing built, checked, measured or written in two places. Look inside this session and across the sessions (ListAgents, the plan folders and handoffs this work reads): a demo whose code is thrown away and the app after it, two tools for one measurement, a session that repeats the one under it, two sessions on one file.
   - It need not happen: review rounds that stopped converging (a round finds as much as the round before, or finds only a document that contradicts itself), polish of something he has not seen yet, detail that will be written again anyway.
   - It belongs at another time: the full spec of a piece that is not the next to be built (`working-style.md`, Requests: a spec is written right before its piece is built), a check that belongs on the built unit and not on the paper, polish before he approved the direction.
3. Read the sources now, not from memory, and keep them apart. His own rules that already answer this: `working-style.md` (Second model: review rounds converge; Requests: the spec right before the build; UI and product work: an app is built once) and `model-routing.md` (Who does the work). What Anthropic publishes on working with Claude Code and with agents: start from `webget https://code.claude.com/docs/en/best-practices` and `webget https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/building-effective-agents`. For each, say what the work follows and where it drifted, with the sentence quoted and its URL. Anthropic publishes recommendations, not rules that bind him: say that in those words when he asks about "the rules of Anthropic".
4. Act on what is yours, and lose nothing he asked for. Finish what is already running. Do not open another round of a kind that step 2 found wasted. Drop your half of a duplicate after the other owner confirmed it holds the whole (SendMessage; `/nadavai:my-other` for a session you cannot reach). Move a piece to the time it belongs to, written where it will be found then (the build plan, the unit's brief). What changes something he decided is his (`working-style.md`, Requests): the review rounds he set for money, messages to people, data and official filings, a "continue until a reviewer approves" he picked, a demo he asked to keep polishing. For those, give your own position and its reason first, then one AskUserQuestion with the recommended answer first. He answered such a dialog that day with «מה אתה חושב שנכון?»: a list of options with no position is not an answer to this prompt.
5. A lesson that would have saved this in other projects goes to the session that keeps the rules, as a proposal (he asked four sessions for that on 2026-11-14). Read `~/.omc/handoffs/` for a method file that already holds the lesson, and add to it instead of writing the same lesson again; otherwise write `~/.omc/handoffs/<project>-method-<date>.md` with his words, the numbers, what works, what is doubled, the sources and the proposed change. Then tell that session where the file is (SendMessage, or `/nadavai:my-other` when it runs under another profile). No rule or skill in `~/nadavai` is changed from here.
6. Reply in everyday Hebrew (`working-style.md`), short enough to read in a minute: the verdict in one line; the numbers; what carries into the product; what is doubled, not needed or at the wrong time; what the sources say, with their links; what you changed just now; your recommendation; what is his to decide; and how sure you are, with the check that would settle it (`candor.md`).
